How to use this handout
This handout is designed to help you move from broad megatrends to actionable trend theses. Use
it as a repeatable workflow for research, discussion, and idea generation.
A simple classroom loop
• Map: megatrend -> subtrend -> local effects (customers, regulation, infrastructure).
• Instrument: choose 3-7 measurable signals to track each subtrend.
• Stress-test: write disconfirming evidence and scenario signposts.
• Translate: derive opportunity patterns (who pays, what changes, what breaks).
The meta-insight for 2026-2035
Track the new scarcity stack: electricity + compute + critical minerals + trust.
Most second-order opportunities sit at the intersections of these constraints.

Executive summary
Four megatrends remain the best high-level map, but the most important dynamics are their
interactions. Fragmentation changes the global operating model; climate transition adds physical
and financial constraints; technology moves from tools to autonomy; demographics reshape labor,
care, and trust.
What is structurally different now
• Globalization is shifting from optimize to secure + comply + duplicate.
• Resilience (energy, supply chains, infrastructure) becomes a core cost category.
• AI value shifts from model quality alone to workflow integration, governance, and reliability.
• Aging societies face labor scarcity, while youth-bulge regions face a jobs race.

Megatrend 1: Geopolitical and economic
fragmentation
The global economy is moving from a single, integrated operating environment to multiple rule-sets,
regional supply networks, and heavier use of economic tools as strategic instruments (export
controls, subsidies, screening, standards).
Key subtrends
• Transactional middle powers: issue-by-issue coalitions and shifting alignments.
• Supply chain regionalization: near-shoring, friend-shoring, and resilience premiums.
• Weaponized interdependence: export controls, sanctions, and investment screening as design
constraints.
• Industrial policy competition: subsidies and local content rules reshape capex decisions.
• Governance workarounds: mini-lateral deals and private standards fill multilateral gaps.
Signals to track
• Trade-restrictive coverage in monitoring reports and frequency of new measures.
• Export control rule updates, controlled categories, and licensing timelines.
• Greenfield FDI announcements and capacity builds in connector geographies.
• Standards divergence across blocs (AI, cybersecurity, ESG, product safety).
Opportunity patterns
• Compliance-native products: audit trails, geo-fenced features, and modular architectures.
• Supply chain visibility: provenance, tier-n mapping, and disruption simulations.
• Industrial policy translation: tooling that helps firms qualify, report, and audit programs.
Discussion prompts
• Where in your industry does compliance become a product feature rather than a back-office
cost?
• If supply chains must be duplicated, what becomes the scarcest input: parts, permits, capital,
or talent?
• What would it take to provide credible provenance for a product at tier-n (not just tier-1)?

Megatrend 2: Climate emergency and the great
transition
The transition is not only decarbonization. It is also adaptation, critical minerals security, and a
financial system repricing risk through disclosure, insurance, and capital costs.
Key subtrends
• Adaptation economy: resilience capex grows (heat, flood, water, grid hardening).
• New geopolitics of minerals: mining, refining, and recycling capacity become strategic.
• Climate risk in finance: disclosures, transition plans, and liability pressures.
• Circular economy imperative: repairability, durability, and product passports.
Signals to track
• Adaptation finance gap measures and growth in resilience spending.
• Insurance pricing and coverage availability in high-risk regions.
• Critical mineral concentration metrics and export restrictions.
• Repairability and durability requirements in major markets.
Opportunity patterns
• Resilience-as-a-service: engineering + monitoring + finance/insurance partnerships.
• Traceability and material intelligence: compliance plus supply security.
• Distributed repair infrastructure: diagnostics, parts logistics, and certified networks.
Discussion prompts
• Which climate risks are becoming price signals (insurance, capital costs) in your region?
• What assets are at risk of becoming stranded - and what replaces them (retrofit, repurpose,
retire)?
• Where could circularity (repair, reuse, recycling) reduce dependence on fragile supply chains?

Megatrend 3: Accelerating technological disruption
Technology is shifting from tools to autonomy. Agentic AI, robotics, biotech, and spatial computing
converge, while physical constraints (electricity, chips, cooling) and governance constraints
(regulation, trust) shape adoption.
Key subtrends
• Agentic AI: systems that plan and act across workflows, not just generate content.
• Compute and infrastructure arms race: chips, data centers, power, and grid queues.
• AI governance and safety: evaluation, monitoring, and auditability become mandatory in many
contexts.
• Bio-digital convergence: AI-accelerated R&D;, lab automation, and personalized medicine.
• Spatial computing and digital twins: simulation and training become routine in regulated
industries.
Signals to track
• Enterprise spend shifts from pilots to governed, production-grade agent platforms.
• Data center power demand growth, interconnection delays, and siting constraints.
• Regulatory milestones (phased AI rules, enforcement guidance, audits).
• R&D; cycle time reductions in drug discovery and lab automation adoption.
Opportunity patterns
• Agent reliability infrastructure: sandboxing, permissions, rollback, simulation testing.
• Energy-aware compute: scheduling and optimization around grid and carbon constraints.
• Authenticity and provenance: verification for synthetic media and digital commerce.
Discussion prompts
• What workflow would you automate first if agents were reliable and permissioned - and what
stops you today?
• Where does electricity availability (not model quality) become the binding constraint for AI
adoption?
• Which decisions in your domain require provable outputs (audit trails, citations,
reproducibility)?

Megatrend 4: Demographic divergence and societal
shifts
The world is splitting into aging, low-fertility regions with labor scarcity and youth-bulge regions
where job creation determines stability. In parallel, trust is fragmenting and well-being is becoming
an economic variable.
Key subtrends
• Silver economy: healthcare, care coordination, accessible housing, and longevity services.
• Labor shortage era: productivity tools and automation to augment scarce workers.
• Youth bulge and jobs race: skills-to-income pathways, fintech, and SME enablement.
• Crisis of trust and insularity: smaller trusted communities, creator influence, verification demand.
• Redefinition of well-being: evidence-based mental health, chronic care prevention, and financial
resilience.
Signals to track
• Vacancy rates in care, health, skilled trades, and education.
• Policy shifts on retirement, migration, and caregiver support.
• Youth NEET rates and formal job creation pace by region.
• Trust metrics and adoption of verification tools in media/commerce.
Opportunity patterns
• Care infrastructure platforms: scheduling, documentation automation, remote monitoring.
• Skills-to-income rails: credentialing linked to verified work outcomes.
• Trust brokers: reputation, dispute resolution, and authenticity tooling for communities.
Discussion prompts
• Which services break first under labor scarcity - and what is the minimum viable automation?
• Where can better care coordination reduce total cost (and caregiver burnout) without lowering
quality?
• What would it take to make trust portable across platforms (identity, reputation, verification)?

Signal dashboard: 25 indicators to track
Assign each student team one megatrend and require: (1) five signals, (2) a target range or
threshold, (3) a disconfirming signal, and (4) a monthly update cadence.
Fragmentation
• Trade-restrictive coverage and new measures frequency (WTO monitoring).
• Export control updates and entity list changes (semiconductors, compute, tools).
• FDI shifts into connector geographies (manufacturing, logistics, energy).
• Dispute settlement reform progress vs stagnation (rule enforcement).
• Industrial policy awards and local content rules by sector.
Climate transition
• Adaptation finance gap and resilience procurement growth.
• Insured catastrophe losses and premium / coverage availability changes.
• Critical mineral concentration, price volatility, and export restrictions.
• Grid interconnection queue length and transmission buildout pace.
• Repairability and durability regulations (application dates and scope).
Tech disruption
• Enterprise move from chat pilots to governed agent platforms.
• Data center power demand growth and siting constraints.
• AI regulation milestones, standards, and enforcement actions.
• Cyber incidents in critical infrastructure and supply chain security posture.
• R&D; cycle time reductions in bio/AI and lab automation adoption.
Demographics and society
• Old-age dependency ratios and labor participation trends.
• Vacancy rates in care/health/skilled trades and wage pressure.
• Youth NEET rates, formal job creation, and SME formation rates.
• Trust and insularity metrics; verification tool adoption.
• Wellness economy spending mix and evidence-based outcomes adoption.
